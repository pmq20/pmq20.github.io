AAAI 2020: notes from the Thirty-Fourth AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence New York, February 2020 · notes in Chinese, 14 pages · LaTeX source A day-by-day log of six days of tutorials, workshops and technical sessions, centered on graph neural networks and differential-equation models. Corrections (2026). In 1(a), GraphSAGE, GAT and MPNN are spatial (message-passing) methods, not spectral filters. In 1(g)–(h), Real NVP is an affine-coupling extension of NICE rather than a continuous-time version, and of the flows listed only GraphNVP generates graphs.

On the Anatomy of MCMC-Based Maximum Likelihood Learning of Energy-Based Models Comments on Nijkamp, Hill, Han, Zhu and Wu (AAAI 2020) · April 2020 · notes in Chinese, 5 pages · LaTeX source · Slides (English) · Paper Open questions the paper leaves (why short-run Langevin chains rarely converge in practice), replications of its three experiments from the authors’ released code, and the toy densities reconstructed from that code. Corrections (2026). The paper’s sampler is unadjusted Langevin, which has no Metropolis–Hastings accept/reject step (item 3), and in overdamped Langevin dynamics the drag balances the force −∇U plus noise rather than equaling the energy gradient (item 4). The data noise in item 7 has covariance 0.015²I, not I; good long-run samples are consistent with convergence but do not prove it.
  3. Factorized Inference in Deep Markov Models for Incomplete Multimodal Time Series Comments on Tan, Soh and Ong (AAAI 2020) · April 2020 · notes in Chinese, 11 pages · LaTeX source · Slides (English) · Paper Derives, step by step, the smoothing posteriors the paper states without proof, factorized over time and modality; reconstructs the datasets from the authors’ code; and attempts to reproduce the noisy-spiral and Weizmann human-action experiments. Corrections (2026). In item 3, the product of marginals p(xt+1:T) ∏m p(xtm) is not the joint p(xt:T); the step holds only up to a factor constant in zt. In item 6(c), Lmatch enforces the model’s assumption of a single time-invariant prior p(z); it does not correct sampling error. My replication of the noisy-spiral experiment did not succeed.

Neural Dynamics on Complex Networks Comments on Zang and Wang (KDD 2020; arXiv 2019) · April 2020 · notes in Chinese, 7 pages · LaTeX source · Slides (English) · Paper Continuous-depth graph networks built on neural ODEs: the diffusion operator Φ(α) and its link to Kipf and Welling’s normalized adjacency, a derivation of the adjoint method, and the node-classification and synthetic-network set-ups rebuilt from the paper and code. Corrections (2026). The Planetoid Cora file has 10,556 directed arcs that are already symmetric, i.e. 5,278 undirected edges; 5,429 is the count in the original LINQS release. Kipf and Welling’s remark that edges need not encode similarity concerns Laplacian regularization, not GCN or NDCN. The adjoint derivation omits the boundary conditions and the final gradient dL/dθ = aθ(t0).
  5. Graph Neural Ordinary Differential Equations Comments on Poli, Massaroli, Park, Yamashita, Asama and Park (arXiv 2019) · April 2020 · notes in Chinese, 4 pages · LaTeX source · Slides (English) · Paper Reads the model through the relational inductive biases of Battaglia et al., and asks why node embeddings diverge here when deep graph convolutional networks are known to over-smooth. Corrections (2026). The central argument of item 4 is mistaken. The DGL Cora graph the authors used is symmetric, so directedness cannot explain the diverging trajectories; Li et al.’s convergence result concerns a fixed operator applied indefinitely, whereas a GCDE has learned weights and a nonlinearity and runs for a finite time. The activation is Softplus, not Softmax; Li et al.’s limit is proportional to D1/21; and the paper’s Table 1 shows no gain from the ODE formulation alone over a tuned GCN.
  6. How Powerful are Graph Neural Networks? Comments on Xu, Hu, Leskovec and Jegelka (ICLR 2019) · April 2020 · notes in Chinese, 3 pages · LaTeX source · Slides (English) · Paper Expressive power bounded by the Weisfeiler–Lehman test, a shorter route to the paper’s Lemma 4 through countability (Rudin, Principles of Mathematical Analysis, Theorem 2.13), and what the bound means for neighborhood aggregation. Corrections (2026). GIN is the most expressive only among neighborhood-aggregation GNNs with countable features, matching but not exceeding the 1-WL test. Treating bounded multisets as 𝒳n (item 3) needs padding with a dummy element and a fixed order, which is what the paper’s longer proof supplies.
